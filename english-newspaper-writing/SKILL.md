# SKILL: English Newspaper Writing for Astronomy Research

## Purpose

This skill produces **clear, accurate, newspaper-style articles** about
astronomy research for **well educated general readers**, assuming an
audience of college graduates who are interested in science but are not
professional astronomers.

The goal is to explain why a recent astronomy result matters, what was
actually found, how researchers know it, and what remains uncertain,
without exaggeration or specialist overload.

---

## Scope

This skill applies to English-language newspaper or magazine-style
articles about astronomy, astrophysics, planetary science, space missions,
observatories, and related research topics of recent public interest.

Supported use cases include:

* Writing a complete newspaper-style article
* Rewriting technical astronomy material for educated public readers
* Creating article outlines, headlines, standfirsts, and section plans
* Editing drafts for clarity, scientific accuracy, tone, and structure
* Explaining a recent paper, mission result, image release, discovery, or controversy

Technical research papers, grant proposals, press releases, and children's
science writing are **out of scope** unless explicitly requested.

---

## Core Workflow

### 1. Verify the News Context

Astronomy topics of recent public interest are time-sensitive. Before
writing, verify the current state of the story using reliable sources.

Prefer:

* The primary research paper, preprint, or conference abstract
* Official pages from missions, observatories, universities, journals, or agencies
* Reputable science desks or specialist astronomy news outlets for public context

Use source material to establish:

* What is new
* Who did the work
* Where it was published or announced
* The date of the paper, observation, image release, or announcement
* The main caveats, uncertainties, and competing interpretations

Do not treat a press release headline as the result. Trace the claim back
to the paper or official technical source whenever possible.

---

### 2. Article Structure

Use a newspaper-style structure with a clear news lead and strong
explanatory flow.

Default structure:

1. **Headline**: Specific, accurate, and readable; avoid clickbait.
2. **Standfirst**: One sentence giving the discovery and its significance.
3. **Lead paragraph**: State the news in plain English: what happened,
   why it matters, and what the reader should understand first.
4. **Nut graf**: Explain the broader scientific context and stakes.
5. **Evidence section**: Describe the observation, instrument, method, or
   dataset that supports the result.
6. **Human and institutional context**: Identify researchers, missions,
   observatories, or collaborations when relevant.
7. **Caveats and limits**: Make uncertainty visible without weakening true
   findings.
8. **Why it matters**: Connect the finding to larger astronomical questions.
9. **Ending**: Close with the next observation, open question, or future
   mission rather than a vague inspirational line.

For shorter articles, compress these elements while preserving the same
logic.

---

### 3. Writing Style and Tone

Write in polished newspaper English:

* Clear, direct, and graceful
* Serious but not dry
* Explanatory without sounding like a textbook
* Concrete before abstract
* Neutral about claims under debate
* Accessible to a college-educated non-specialist

Avoid:

* Hype, awe-driven cliches, and sensational language
* Anthropomorphic descriptions of astronomical objects
* Overloaded technical terminology
* Long chains of subordinate clauses
* Rhetorical questions
* Vague phrases such as "mysteries of the universe" unless made specific

Define necessary technical terms on first use. Keep definitions short and
embedded in the prose.

Preferred:

> The observation suggests that the planet's atmosphere contains water
> vapour, but it does not by itself show that the planet is habitable.

Disallowed:

> Scientists have found another Earth where life may be waiting to be discovered.

---

### 4. Scientific Accuracy

All scientific claims must be accurate and appropriately qualified.

Clearly distinguish:

* Direct observations
* Model-dependent inferences
* Statistical evidence
* Hypotheses and interpretations
* Speculation or future possibilities

State limitations plainly. If a result is preliminary, contested, based on
a small sample, or dependent on a model, say so.

Do not invent numbers, quotes, dates, institutional affiliations, or paper
details. If exact values are not available from reliable sources, either
omit them or say they were not specified.

---

### 5. Numbers, Scale, and Comparisons

Use numbers when they improve understanding, but do not crowd the article.

Guidelines:

* Convert technical quantities into reader-friendly comparisons when useful
* Keep original scientific units when precision matters
* Avoid false precision in rounded explanatory prose
* Explain extreme scales through comparison, not spectacle

When using analogies, make their limits clear if the analogy could mislead.

---

### 6. Quotes and Attribution

Use direct quotations only when provided by the user or available from a
source that can be cited. Do not fabricate quotes.

Attribution should be precise:

* Name the scientist, institution, mission, collaboration, or paper
* Attribute claims to researchers when they are not settled facts
* Mention publication venue or preprint status when relevant

If no direct quotes are available, write without quotes rather than
inventing them.

---

### 7. Headline and Standfirst

Headlines should be accurate, compact, and specific.

Prefer:

* "Webb observations sharpen the case for a carbon-rich exoplanet atmosphere"
* "A new radio map reveals how gas flows through the Milky Way"

Avoid:

* "Scientists stunned by impossible discovery"
* "This changes everything we know about the universe"

The standfirst should add the main significance or caveat, not simply
repeat the headline.

---

### 8. Links and Sources

When sources are used, include a concise source list unless the user asks
for prose only.

Good source types include:

* Journal article or arXiv paper
* NASA, ESA, ISRO, JAXA, ESO, NRAO, ALMA, NOIRLab, or mission pages
* University or observatory release
* Reputable newspaper or science-news report for public context

Do not overload the article with inline links unless the user requests a
web-ready version. For print-style prose, keep links in a source note.

---

### 9. Editing and Revision

When editing a draft:

1. Preserve the user's central argument and intended audience.
2. Correct scientific inaccuracies and flag uncertain claims.
3. Improve the lead, paragraph order, transitions, and ending.
4. Remove hype and vague language.
5. Tighten sentences while preserving nuance.

If a claim is unsupported or likely wrong, do not silently smooth it over;
fix it or note the issue.

---

## Explicit Non-Goals

This skill must **not**:

* Write a specialist research article
* Produce a press release or promotional copy
* Oversimplify a result into a misleading claim
* Invent quotes, data, publication details, or expert reactions
* Use sensational, clickbait, or "breakthrough" language without strong justification
* Present speculation about alien life, habitability, or cosmic significance as fact

---

## Expected User Inputs

Typical inputs may include:

* The astronomy topic or paper
* Desired word count
* Intended publication style
* Whether sources and links should be included
* Draft text to revise
* Preferred headline style

If the user does not specify length, assume a concise newspaper article of
about 800-1,000 words.

Ask for clarification only when the missing information materially changes
the article. Otherwise, make conservative assumptions and state them
briefly.

---

## Output Guarantee

Every response produced by this skill must be:

* Scientifically accurate
* Suitable for college-educated non-specialists
* Clear, readable, and newspaper-like
* Honest about uncertainty
* Free of fabricated quotes or unsupported claims
* Focused on recent astronomy research or public-interest astronomy news
