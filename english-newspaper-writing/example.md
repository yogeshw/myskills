Use this structure when writing an astronomy newspaper article for
well educated general readers.

# Model Article Skeleton

## Headline

Use a concrete, accurate headline that names the result without overstating it.

Good:

`Astronomers find a surprisingly massive black hole in the early universe`

Avoid:

`Impossible black hole discovery shocks scientists`

## Standfirst

One sentence that gives the result and its significance or caveat.

Example:

`The finding adds to evidence that some black holes grew faster than simple models of early galaxy formation predict.`

## Lead

Start with the news, not with a broad statement about the universe.

Example:

`Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope have identified a distant galaxy whose central black hole appears unusually massive for the young age of the universe, raising fresh questions about how the first supermassive black holes formed.`

## Nut Graf

Explain why the result matters in the larger research problem.

Example:

`Supermassive black holes sit at the centres of many large galaxies today, but researchers are still trying to understand how some of them reached enormous masses within the first billion years after the Big Bang.`

## Evidence Paragraph

Describe what was measured and how the inference was made.

Example:

`The team used infrared observations to study light stretched by cosmic expansion. Features in the spectrum allowed them to estimate the galaxy's distance and infer the presence of rapidly moving gas near a compact central object, a common sign of an actively feeding black hole.`

## Caveat Paragraph

State limits clearly and early enough that the article remains honest.

Example:

`The result does not mean existing models are wrong on their own. The inferred mass depends on assumptions about the gas around the black hole, and more observations will be needed to test whether this object is unusual or part of a broader early population.`

## Ending

Close with the next scientific step.

Example:

`Future Webb observations, combined with measurements from large ground-based telescopes, should help determine whether these early black holes grew from massive stellar remnants, direct-collapse gas clouds, or a mixture of several pathways.`

# Paragraph Rules

* Keep most paragraphs between two and four sentences.
* Put the main claim near the start of each paragraph.
* Explain one scientific idea at a time.
* Use technical terms only when they earn their place.
* Attribute unsettled interpretations to researchers.
* Do not fabricate quotes; write clean reported prose when quotes are unavailable.
